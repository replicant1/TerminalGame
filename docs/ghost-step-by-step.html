The ghost, one tick at a time

The game's own arena — twenty-nine cells by nineteen, the size GameViewModel builds — with the ghost moving one cell per tick and saying what it did and why. It is not hunting. It carries straight on until the cell ahead is wall, and only then draws a random number. Press Step for one tick. The player stands still here, so anything that happens to them happens by accident, which is the whole point.

The rule, in three lines. Try the cell ahead; if it is corridor, move there and keep going. Otherwise collect the sides that are corridor and are not the way it came, draw one of them at random, and turn. If there are none — a dead end — reverse. That is GameViewModel._advance_ghost in full, and there is nothing else: no target, no distance, no path.

Watch the reversal counter stay at zero. Reversing is the only branch that needs a dead end, and braiding removed every dead end from this maze before play started — see carving and braiding, one step at a time. The branch is still written, because a maze built by hand in a test need not be braided, and because a ghost that could not reverse would be stuck rather than slow.

Where the randomness goes. A corridor costs nothing: carrying straight on draws no random number at all. The draw happens only where the ghost is blocked, and only among the ways out that are not the way back — usually one or two of them. That is why a seeded game is reproducible, and why the ghost reads as moving with purpose while having none.

What this page leaves out. The player. In a real game they move a whole cell per arrow key, they eat the pills, and a capture can happen from either side: the ghost walking onto them, in tick, or them walking onto the ghost, in on_direction. Here they stand where farthest_open put them and wait. The endings are collected in how a game begins and ends.

Seeds. The maze and the ghost's turns come from two Mersenne Twisters, each seeded the way CPython's random module seeds its own — which is exactly what GameViewModel(seed=n) holds. So this arena, these two starting cells and this route are what Python produces for the same seed, tick for tick, as long as nobody presses an arrow key.
